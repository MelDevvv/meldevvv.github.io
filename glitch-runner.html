01 // PROJECT OVERVIEW

BUILT TO FRUSTRATE.
DESIGNED TO REACT.

Glitch Runner started as a gameplay prototype and developed into an experiment in player frustration, timing, audio and environmental interaction.

The core concept is a rage-bait platformer where failure is deliberately part of the experience. The player is required to learn the level, understand its timing and react quickly to obstacles while the game continually challenges their expectations.

A major focus of the project was connecting the gameplay to music and rhythm. The environment and gameplay systems were designed around the audio rather than treating music as something separate from the game.

This allowed me to experiment with how sound can influence the player's perception of movement, timing and progression, while also using visual effects and environmental changes to make the game world feel responsive.

02 // GAMEPLAY

THE GAMEPLAY LOOP

The prototype combines difficult platforming with timing-based gameplay and environmental interaction. The goal was to make movement feel simple to learn while creating a much harder experience to master.

SYSTEM 01

RAGE-BAIT PLATFORMING

Precision platforming designed around failure, retries and learning. Obstacles and level layouts are structured to create moments where the player knows exactly why they failed and immediately wants to try again.

SYSTEM 02

MUSIC-DRIVEN GAMEPLAY

Gameplay and environmental behaviour are connected to the music, creating rhythm-based moments where timing and audio become important parts of the player's interaction with the level.

SYSTEM 03

REACTIVE ENVIRONMENT

The stage responds to the soundtrack through movement, visual changes and gameplay events, helping create the feeling that the level itself is reacting to the player and music.

SYSTEM 04

PLAYER MOVEMENT

Responsive movement and camera controls provide the foundation for the platforming systems, with the controls designed to remain predictable even when the level becomes increasingly difficult.

SYSTEM 05

INTERACTIVE OBJECTS

Interactive objects are used to turn the environment into part of the gameplay. Objects can be approached, manipulated and repositioned by the player.

SYSTEM 06

AUDIO & FEEDBACK

Extensive audio implementation reinforces movement, interactions and gameplay events while helping the player understand what is happening around them.

03 // INTERACTION
THE WORLD ISN'T STATIC.

One of the technical experiments in the project involved building a world containing floating barrels and interactive objects. Rather than being purely decorative, these objects can be picked up and moved around by the player.

The player can navigate the environment using WASD movement and camera controls, approach objects and manipulate them within the world. This provided an opportunity to experiment with object interaction, movement, physics and player-to-environment relationships.

The system also helped explore how relatively simple interactions can make a level feel more dynamic and responsive.

04 // AUDIO & RHYTHM

SOUND BECOMES GAMEPLAY

Audio was treated as a core gameplay component rather than simply background music. The project explores how rhythm, sound effects and visual reactions can work together to create stronger feedback.

RHYTHM EVENTS
